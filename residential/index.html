No Surprises

Honest, Upfront Pricing

Good water heater service starts with knowing exactly what you're dealing with. Newton Water Heater offers honest, upfront pricing for every home, handling everything from a failed heating element to a complete tank replacement. Every visit begins with a thorough assessment of your unit, including its age, capacity, fuel type, venting, and valve and tank condition, followed by a clear recommendation and a written price before any work starts.

The problem with water heater failures is that many of them develop out of sight. Minerals settle into a crust on the bottom of the tank, the anode rod gets eaten away a little at a time, and once it's gone, the tank itself starts to corrode. When hot water comes out rusty, the tank rumbles, or a small drip appears at the base, get it looked at while it's still a simple repair.

Do you know how old your water heater is? The serial number on the label usually tells us. Once a tank passes the 10-year mark, it's worth scheduling a replacement before the current one forces the issue. For reference, a 40 to 50 gallon gas tank typically runs $1,400–$2,800 installed, and an electric tank typically runs $1,100–$2,400, depending on venting, code updates, and access. We will tell you plainly whether yours has years left or is running on borrowed time.

Maintenance Plans

Water Heater Maintenance That Adds Years

Routine maintenance is the most reliable way to keep hot water flowing year-round. Regular service prevents failures before they start, clears out sediment, and catches small faults while they're still cheap to fix. At Newton Water Heater, every maintenance visit is built around your specific heater, covering a full tank flush, an anode rod inspection, a test of the temperature and pressure relief valve, a burner and flue check on gas units, element and thermostat testing on electric units, and a temperature check at the tap.

Every maintenance plan we write is a little different. An older tank needs a closer look than a new heater, a tankless unit on hard water needs annual descaling to keep its heat exchanger clear, and a large family works the tank far harder than a couple does. We set up each visit around your heater, your water, and how your family uses it, so every tune-up helps keep the unit efficient and protects the equipment you've already paid for.

Homeowners are often surprised how much a regular maintenance schedule pays off. A clean tank heats water faster and more quietly, a healthy anode rod protects the steel from corrosion, and a working relief valve protects the whole house. Most families find that a maintenance plan costs far less than replacing a water heater even a few years early.

Right-Sized Hot Water

A Water Heater Sized for Your Actual Household

When it comes to water heaters, bigger is not automatically better. What matters for a tank is the first-hour rating, how many gallons of hot water it can deliver in one busy hour, while for a tankless unit it's the GPM it can hold at your incoming water temperature. We don't simply copy the size of the old unit. We look at how many people live in the home, how many bathrooms you have, plus large draws such as a jetted tub or high-capacity washer.

Needs change over time. A new baby, a teenager, or an in-law suite can overwhelm the tank that once kept up just fine. A tankless unit heats water on demand and never runs out as long as you stay within the rated flow, while heat pump water heaters draw far less electricity than a standard electric tank. Whether you're comparing tank and tankless, or gas and electric, we'll lay out the honest pros and cons in installed price, operating cost, venting and gas line needs, and installation work.

Common Problems

Hot Water Problems We Solve Every Week

The majority of repair calls we get come down to a handful of familiar problems. The water never gets hot. The pilot lights and then goes out a minute later. The hot water runs out fast, the tank rumbles and pops, or a tankless unit starts flashing an error code. Add rusty water, sulfur smells, and dripping relief valves, and you have the full list. We fix every one of them, starting with the exact part that failed.

Some problems are mostly a nuisance, while others pose real risks. A burned-out element simply leaves you with lukewarm water, but a relief valve that keeps discharging is warning you that temperature or pressure inside the tank is too high, and a thermostat set above 120°F can scald a child in seconds. A gas smell near the heater means leave the house first and call the gas utility from outside, then call us. We match the fix to the failure: elements and thermostats on electric tanks, thermocouples, flame sensors, and gas valves on gas units, a thermal expansion tank where water pressure is spiking, and leak repair on drain valves, fittings, and relief lines.
