How Waltham Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is simple maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve annually to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and cuts down on scale. Tankless owners should plan on a descaling flush once a year in hard-water areas.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the manufacture date, and its age tells you a lot. Most tank heaters make it to 8 to 12 years, heat pump models 10 to 15, and well-maintained tankless units around 20. If your heater is past that range and also shows rusty water, rumbling, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and less stressful than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

Small problems rarely stay small when they're ignored. Consider a spent anode rod: replacing it is a short, affordable repair, but if nobody replaces it, the water starts eating the tank itself, and just like that a minor fix has become a full replacement. That's why we ask homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves money and spares you a flooded utility room.
