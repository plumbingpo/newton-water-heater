When Your Water Heater Fails in Cambridge

Call us about a problem and a real person picks up, not a recording. The emergency calls we answer tend to follow a pattern. On gas heaters, it's a pilot that won't stay lit, a burner that won't ignite, or a gas control valve that has failed. On electric heaters, it's a burned-out element, a faulty high-limit switch, or a breaker that won't stay on. On every type, it's leaks at the base, the drain valve, or the fittings, plus relief valves that discharge. Because our trucks carry thermocouples, elements, thermostats, gas valves, relief valves, and fittings, most of these are wrapped up on the first trip, with the price agreed before any work begins.

A little caution while you wait for us can head off a much bigger mess. For a leak, turn off the cold supply valve at the top of the tank, then switch off the breaker for an electric model or set the gas control to "off" on a gas model. Clear stored items away from the heater and put down towels to slow the spread. Never ignore the smell of gas: get everyone out and call the gas utility from outside.

Getting More Years From Your Water Heater in Cambridge

Routine maintenance makes all the difference between a water heater that runs for years and one that fails early. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: an annual sediment flush, an anode inspection every few years, a quick relief valve test once a year, a thermostat set near 120 degrees, and annual descaling for tankless units on hard water, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Take a look at the age of your water heater. The serial number on the label usually includes the month and year it was built, and its age says a lot about what comes next. Most tank heaters reach 8 to 12 years, heat pump units 10 to 15, and regularly descaled tankless units about 20. If your heater is beyond that range and is also showing discolored water, popping sounds, or moisture at the base, it's usually due for a planned replacement, which is almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than a rushed emergency install once the tank fails.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or a missing expansion tank, both modest fixes. The same drip left alone for a season can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Common Water Heater Problems in Cambridge

Water heaters in and around Cambridge face a familiar set of stresses. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be a real factor: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, forcing the burner or lower element to work harder, and that's what causes the popping and rumbling you may hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why annual descaling is worth keeping up with where the water is hard.

Aging equipment is the other half of the story. A tank-style water heater usually runs 8 to 12 years, and by then the anode rod is often eaten away, leaving the steel exposed to corrosion. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and heating elements and thermostats on electric ones.

Code is another piece. Swapping out an older heater frequently means updating the installation along the way: a thermal expansion tank if the plumbing is a closed system, a T&P discharge line piped to an approved spot, a flue that drafts correctly on gas units, plus straps and a pan where required. Well water brings its own challenges, such as extra sediment, iron staining, and the sulfur odor that sometimes develops when certain water chemistry reacts with a standard anode rod. All of these can be fixed, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a quick diagnostic visit catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
